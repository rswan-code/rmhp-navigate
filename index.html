45–54 COHORT — COMPOUND BURDEN

45–54 carries the highest SDoH need in the program — 5.6 distinct needs per member on average

The 45–54 age cohort (18% of enrolled) presents with the highest social burden in the program. At 5.6 average SDoH needs per member, these members are managing multiple simultaneous challenges — economic instability, social isolation, health literacy gaps, and care access barriers — that compound their risk of avoidable utilization and missed quality measures. The 55–64 cohort, meanwhile, carries the highest PHQ-4 distress score (4.8), sitting at the lower boundary of moderate distress. Both cohorts are among RMHP's highest-value populations for Navigate intervention.

Talking Points — Program Overview

  • 502 members enrolled in 12 weeks demonstrates the Navigate model is reaching RMHP's priority population at scale. March's 85 enrollments reflect a half-month launch; April through May show the program at full operating pace with 168 and 185 new enrollments respectively
  • 0% bad phone numbers is a data quality signal — RMHP's eligibility file has clean, dialable contact information for this population. That directly translates into higher outreach conversion rates compared to plans with significant contact data gaps
  • 72% app enrollment alongside 28% CSC-only shows the Navigate model is reaching members across both digital and non-digital engagement preferences. The CSC serves as the safety net for members who don't engage through the app — no member is left behind by digital-first design
  • The 45–54 cohort's 5.6 average SDoH needs per member is the most important population intelligence in the table — these are RMHP's highest-complexity, highest-cost members outside of care management, and Pyx is the only program currently generating this data at the member level

SOCIAL & COMMUNITY — 73% OF SCREENED MEMBERS

Social isolation is the dominant barrier — present in nearly 3 of 4 RMHP members screened, and directly driving the 41% loneliness rate at intake

Social and Community barriers at 73% are not independent of the UCLA-3 loneliness scores — they are the mechanism. Members who lack social connection and community support are the same members screening lonely, showing PHQ-4 distress, and avoiding care. Economic Stability at 45% compounds this: members managing financial instability alongside social isolation face a compounding set of barriers that standard care management does not reach. These barriers are surfaced through Navigate engagement — not claims data, not prior authorization, not provider referral.

Talking Points — Screening & Loneliness

  • 41% loneliness at intake in a General Medicaid population is a high baseline — this is a population with established social isolation that predicts avoidance of preventive care, missed follow-up appointments, and behavioral health escalation. Pyx is establishing this baseline at enrollment, before any of those downstream events occur
  • The June loneliness-at-onboarding rate of 53% is the most important trend in the data — it means the members being reached now are carrying more acute need than those reached in March and April. Navigate's human-first model is designed for exactly this population; the deepening need is the program working as intended
  • 61% lonely-to-distress crossover means that of the 41% who screen lonely, nearly two-thirds also show PHQ-4 distress. These members are at the front of the behavioral health escalation pipeline — they are the candidates for BH treatment initiation, missed HEDIS quality measures, and avoidable ED utilization if the barriers are not addressed
  • Social & Community at 73% is not a standalone finding — it is the underlying mechanism for nearly every other barrier in the chart. A member who is socially isolated is less likely to know how to navigate care access, less likely to adhere to medication, and less likely to address care gaps. Resolving social isolation is upstream of resolving the downstream clinical barriers
  • Health Literacy at 26% is a critical RMHP quality measure implication — members who don't understand how to use their benefits, navigate the system, or understand care instructions drive the care gap and access barriers. Pyx identifies health literacy need at screening and routes members to navigation support before they miss an appointment
